## Choices made while writing

- **Strong duality for finitely supported laws (`lem:finite`).** The report used Slater's condition
  for a convex program whose variable lives in the (non-closed) cone generated by the moment curve
  $u\mapsto(1,u,\varphi(u))$, with Rockafellar's Theorem 28.2 or Boyd–Vandenberghe §5.2.3. Neither text
  could be read for this paper (the Boyd–Vandenberghe PDF was refused by the server on 2026-11-16),
  so the paper gives a direct proof instead: take a maximiser $\lambda^\star$ of the dual, show by a
  separation argument and an explicit feasible perturbation $\lambda^\star+t(d-\epsilon\lambda^\star)$ that the gradient
  of the dual at $\lambda^\star$ is minus a non-negative combination of the vectors $\gamma(u)=(u,\varphi(u))$ at
  the zeros $u$ of $h_{\lambda^\star}$, and read off an optimal law. Bonus: the optimal law has at most two
  atoms outside the support (zeros of a non-negative quartic), as observed numerically. This proof was
  not independently checked at the time of writing (weak point 1 of the dossier); see the entry below.
- **Threshold with $t\vee3$.** With the house convention ($A_{t+1}$ chosen at the end of round $t$, from
  $t=K\ge2$), $\log\log t$ is negative at $t=2$; the threshold uses $\log(t\vee3)$ and the optimal-arm sum
  becomes $\le2/(3\log^23)+1/\log3\approx1.463\le3/2$.
- **Explicit $n_T(\varepsilon)$.** From $nk\le\ell_T+2\log(1+n)$ and the tangent bound
  $\log y\le\log c+y/c-1$ at $c=4/k$: $n\le\bar n:=2(\ell_T+2\log(4/k))/k$, then
  $n_T\le(\ell_T+2\log(1+\bar n))/k$ (uses $k<1$, true since $\mathcal K\le\mathcal K_\infty\le\log2$).
- **Lower deviation stated at the level $\mu-\delta$** (instead of an infimum over $|\mu'-\mu|\le\delta$): the
  same by monotonicity, and the event is then visibly measurable.
- **Centred moments left out** (the report's "Theorem E"): see `DOSSIER.md`, "Material left out".
- **Style.** The owner's house style of 2026-11-16 (sections 2–2a of the new writing rules): one
  section per result, "Useful lemmas" appendix, one appendix per theorem with a decomposition lemma
  and one lemma per term, every defined quantity with `:=` in its own sentence and an anchor
  (`\dt`), every later use through its macro (`\sy`), defined words through `\term` (law, instance,
  model, suboptimal, consistent, anytime). The links were added by a script after the text was
  written, then checked by hand; uses before the definitions (abstract, introduction, related work)
  are plain. The rules name the last section "Conclusion and open questions", but
  `scripts/certify.py check` requires a section titled exactly "Open questions": the paper has a
  short "Conclusion" section followed by "Open questions".

## Dead ends and remarks

- Mass escaping along the affine group cannot be avoided by restricting the outer variables: for a
  law $P$ with finite fourth moment, $\mathcal K(P,\mu)\to\mathcal K_\infty(\kappa)$ as $\mu\to\infty$ and the near-optimal
  scales $s$ go to infinity; a confinement lemma for empirical laws (open question on computation)
  would need an explicit modulus.
- Lower semicontinuity fails at Dirac masses (for $\kappa\ge3$, $\mathcal N(0,n^{-2})\to\delta_0$ with
  $\mathcal K(\cdot,0)=0$ while $\mathcal K(\delta_0,0)=\mathcal K_\infty$); for $\kappa<3$ a scaled-down symmetric two-point law
  does the same. This is why the regret analysis uses semicontinuity only at the true arm laws.
- [L17, Theorem 9] prints the class of the alternatives with the subscript of the kurtosis of $\nu$;
  read literally, $\nu$ itself would not be in it. The proof builds alternatives of kurtosis at most
  $\kappa_\circ$. The paper restates the weaker reading (alternatives in the class with parameter
  $\kappa_\circ$).

## Numerical experiments (this directory)

Python (numpy, scipy), short runs, one at a time.

- `kinf_tools.py`: the dual value at fixed $(c,s)$ (nested bounded one-dimensional maximisations with
  the closed form of the half-width $L(\lambda_2)$), the profiled value (multi-start Nelder–Mead in
  $(\log(c-\mu),\log s)$ plus a search on $c=\mu$), the direct non-convex primal (SLSQP, two free atoms,
  random restarts), the convex primal at fixed $(c,s)$.
- `table1_kinfty.py` → `table1_out.txt` (seconds): $\mathcal K_\infty(\kappa)$, the bounds
  $\kappa<1/\mathcal K_\infty<\kappa+2$, $1/\mathcal K_\infty-\kappa\to3/2$ ($1.49989$ at $\kappa=10^4$), $\log\frac\kappa{\kappa-1}$, and the
  largest atom of 4-point laws (found $q_\kappa$ to 6 digits). Paper: Table `tab:kinfty`.
- `table2_duality.py` → `table2_out.txt` (a few minutes): (a) dual vs convex primal at fixed $(c,s)$ on
  4 random laws (agree to 7 digits); (b) profiled dual vs direct primal for the uniform law on
  $\{-1,0,1\}$, $\kappa=3$ (agree to 6 digits; optimal $c=\mu$; one far atom); (c) the extremal two-point law.
  Paper: Table `tab:duality`.
- `table3_curves.py` → `table3_out.txt` (about 30 minutes): $\mathcal K(P,\Delta)$ for the standardised
  41-point Gaussian and 21-point uniform laws, $\kappa\in\{3,5\}$. Paper: Table `tab:curves`. With the closed
  form of $L(\lambda_2)$ the small-gap values agree with the refined computation of the report (its Table 4)
  and remove the small underestimate of its Table 3 at $\Delta\le0.1$.

What they show: the dual formula agrees with direct searches; $\mathcal K\approx\Delta^2/(2\sigma^2)$ for small gaps
(ratio $\ge0.99$ at $\Delta\le0.1$), $\mathcal K$ within 20% of $\mathcal K_\infty$ from $\Delta\approx\sigma$ on; the exact coefficient
$\Delta/\mathcal K$ is 1.4–2.1 times Lattimore's bracket on these laws. No certificate: the outer minimisation is
heuristic. KL-UCB-Kurt itself was not simulated.
