## Results

Statements copied from the page images (pp. 3724–3743); the source's notation: $\mathcal A_a$ the
laws on $[a,1]$, $\mathcal A=\mathcal A_{-\infty}$ the laws on $(-\infty,1]$, $\mathrm E(F)$ the
mean, $D(F\|G)$ the Kullback–Leibler divergence, $T_i(n)$ the number of pulls of arm $i$ "through
the first $n$ rounds", $J(n)$ the arm pulled at round $n$, $\hat F_{i,t}$ and $\hat\mu_{i,t}$ the
empirical law and mean of arm $i$ after $t$ pulls, $\hat F_i(n)\equiv\hat F_{i,T_i(n)}$,
$\hat\mu_i(n)\equiv\hat\mu_{i,T_i(n)}$, $\hat\mu^*(n)\equiv\max_i\hat\mu_i(n)$, $\mu^*$ the largest
mean, $\Delta_i\equiv\mu^*-\mu_i$, $\mathcal I_{\mathrm{opt}}\equiv\{j:\mu_j=\mu^*\}$.

- **Definition of $D_{\inf}$ (§2.1).** "$D_{\inf}(F,\mu;\mathcal A_a)\equiv\inf_{G\in\mathcal A_a:\mathrm E(G)>\mu}D(F\|G)$".
- **Algorithm 1 (IMED Policy).** "Initialization: Pull each arm once. Loop: Choose an arm $i$
  minimizing $I_i(n)\equiv T_i(n)D_{\inf}(\hat F_i(n),\hat\mu^*(n);\mathcal A)+\log T_i(n)$, where the
  tie-breaking rule is arbitrary."
- **(2).** "$L(\nu;F,\mu)\equiv\mathrm E_F[\log(1-(X-\mu)\nu)]$,
  $L_{\max}(F,\mu)\equiv\max_{0\le\nu\le\frac1{1-\mu}}L(\nu;F,\mu)$."
- **(3) (DMED).** The list of arms with
  "$T_i(n)D_{\inf}(\hat F_i(n),\hat\mu^*(n);\mathcal A_0)+\log T_i(n)\le\log n$".
- **Proposition 1** (Honda and Takemura, 2010, Theorem 5). "For all $F\in\mathcal A_0$ and $\mu<1$ it
  holds that $D_{\inf}(F,\mu;\mathcal A_0)=L_{\max}(F,\mu)$."
- **Theorem 2.** "Let $a\in[-\infty,1)$ and $F\in\mathcal A_a$ be arbitrary. (i)
  $D_{\inf}(F,\mu;\mathcal A_a)=D_{\inf}(F,\mu;\mathcal A)$. (ii) If $\mu<1$ then
  $D_{\inf}(F,\mu;\mathcal A)=L_{\max}(F,\mu)$."
- **(4)–(6).** "$\nu_i^*\equiv\operatorname{argmax}_{0\le\nu\le\frac1{1-\mu^*}}\mathrm E_{F_i}[\log(1-(X-\mu^*)\nu)]$,
  $\lambda_{i,\mu}\equiv\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}:\mathrm E_{F_i}[(\frac{1-X}{1-\mu})^\lambda]\le1\}$" (4);
  "$\Lambda_i^*(x)\equiv\sup_\lambda\{\lambda x-\log\mathrm E_{F_i}[\mathrm e^{\lambda X}]\}$" (5);
  "$\tilde\Lambda_i^*(x)\equiv\sup_\lambda\{\lambda x-\log\mathrm E_{F_i}[(1-(X-\mu^*)\nu_i^*)^\lambda]\}$" (6).
- **Theorem 3.** "Assume that $\mu^*<1$ and $\mathrm E_{F_j}[\mathrm e^{\lambda X}]<\infty$ in some
  neighborhood of $\lambda=0$ for some $j\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{opt}}$. Then, for any fixed
  $0<\delta<\min_{i:\mu_i<\mu^*}\Delta_i/2$, the expected number of pulls of a suboptimal arm
  $i\notin\mathcal I_{\mathrm{opt}}$ is bounded as
  $$\mathrm E[T_i(n)]\le\frac{\log n}{D_{\inf}(F_i,\mu^*)-\frac{2\delta}{1-\mu^*}}+\frac{1}{1-\mathrm e^{-\tilde\Lambda_i^*(D_{\inf}(F_i,\mu^*)-\frac{\delta}{1-\mu^*})}}+\min_{j\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{opt}}}\Big\{\frac{6\mathrm e}{(1-1/\lambda_{j,\mu^*-\delta})(1-\mathrm e^{-(1-1/\lambda_{j,\mu^*-\delta})\Lambda_j^*(\mu^*-\delta)})^3}\Big\}."$$
  It is followed by the same bound summed with weights $\Delta_i$ for $\mathrm E[\mathcal R(n)]$.
- **Corollary 4.** "Under the assumption of Theorem 3,
  $\mathrm E[\mathcal R(n)]=\sum_{i:\mu_i<\mu^*}\frac{\Delta_i\log n}{D_{\inf}(F_i,\mu^*)}+\mathrm O((\log n)^{10/11})$." (7)
  Proof: $\lambda_{j,\mu^*-\delta}=1+\mathrm O(\delta)$, $\Lambda_i^*(\mu^*-\delta)=\mathrm O(\delta^2)$,
  $\tilde\Lambda_i^*(\cdot)=\mathrm O(\delta^2)$ (shown in Appendix A, not read), then
  $\delta=\mathrm O((\log n)^{-1/11})$.
- **Theorem 5.** "(i) Assume that $\mu^*<1$ and $\mathrm E_{F_i}[\mathrm e^{\lambda X}]<\infty$ in some
  neighborhood of $\lambda=0$ for all $i\in\{1,2,\cdots,K\}$. Then
  $\mathrm E[\mathcal R(n)]=\sum_{i:\mu_i<\mu^*}\frac{\Delta_i\log n}{D_{\inf}(F_i,\mu^*)}+\mathrm O(1)$. (8)
  (ii) Furthermore, if the distribution of each arm has a bounded support then the reminder term
  $\mathrm O(1)$ in (8) can be replaced with $-\mathrm O(\log\log n)$, that is, there exists $C>0$ such
  that for all sufficiently large $n$ $\mathrm E[\mathcal R(n)]\le\sum_{i:\mu_i<\mu^*}\frac{\Delta_i\log n}{D_{\inf}(F_i,\mu^*)}-C\log\log n$." (9)
- **Lemma 6.** "Assume that $\mathrm E(F)<\mu<1$ holds. If $\mathrm E_F[(1-\mu)/(1-X)]<1$ then
  $\nu^*=(1-\mu)^{-1}$ and therefore $\mathrm E_F[1/(1-(X-\mu)\nu^*)]<1$. Otherwise,
  $\nu^*\in(0,(1-\mu)^{-1})$ and $\mathrm E_F[1/(1-(X-\mu)\nu^*)]=1$." Here
  $\nu^*=\nu^*(F,\mu)=\operatorname{argmax}_{0\le\nu\le(1-\mu)^{-1}}L(\nu;F,\mu)$, and (12):
  $L'(\nu;F,\mu)=\frac1\nu\big(1-\mathrm E_F\big[\frac1{1-(X-\mu)\nu}\big]\big)$. The source says
  "Lemma 6 is straightforward from the derivatives (12) and (13)". *Reader's remark (not in the
  source):* in the boundary case $\mathrm E_F[(1-\mu)/(1-X)]=1$ the maximizer is $(1-\mu)^{-1}$ (not
  interior) and the expectation equals 1; only "$\le1$" holds in general for the expectation at the
  maximizer. P004 proves what it needs (its Lemma B.2) and does not cite Lemma 6.
- **Lemma 7.** "$L_{\max}(F,\mu)$ is differentiable in $\mu<\mathrm E(F)$ with
  $\frac{\mathrm dL_{\max}(F,\mu)}{\mathrm d\mu}=\nu^*(F,\mu)\le\frac1{1-\mu}$."
- **Lemma 8.** "Fix arbitrary $\mu,\tilde\mu<1$ and $\epsilon>0$. Then there exists
  $a(\epsilon,\mu,\tilde\mu)$ such that $|L_{\max}(F_{(a)},\mu)-L_{\max}(F,\mu)|\le\epsilon$ for all
  $a\le a(\epsilon,\mu,\tilde\mu)$ and all $F\in\mathcal A$ such that $\mathrm E(F)\ge\tilde\mu$."
  ($F_{(a)}$: the mass of $(-\infty,a)$ moved to $a$.)
- **Proof of Theorem 2(ii), the optimal $G^*$ (p. 3735).** "$G^*(S)\equiv\int_S\frac{1-\mu}{1-x}\mathrm dF+(1-\mathrm E_F[\frac{1-\mu}{1-X}])\mathbb 1[1\in S]$
  if $\mathrm E_F[\frac{1-\mu}{1-X}]\le1$, and $\int_S\frac1{1-(x-\mu)\nu^*}\mathrm dF$ if
  $\mathrm E_F[\frac{1-\mu}{1-X}]>1$. We can see from Lemma 6 that $G^*$ is a probability measure
  such that $\mathrm E(G^*)=\mu$ and $D(F\|G^*)=L(\nu^*;F,\mu)=L_{\max}(F,\mu)$." Then
  $(1-\epsilon)G^*+\epsilon\delta_1$ has mean $>\mu$ and divergence $L_{\max}(F,\mu)-\log(1-\epsilon)$.
- **Proposition 10.** "For any $F\in\mathcal A$, $\mu>\mathrm E(F)$ and $u<D_{\inf}(F,\mu)$,
  $P_F[D_{\inf}(\hat F_t,\mu)\le u]\le\mathrm e^{-t\tilde\Lambda^*(u)}$, where
  $\tilde\Lambda^*(x)=\sup_\lambda\{\lambda x-\mathrm E_F[(1-(X-\mu)\nu^*)^\lambda]\}$ for
  $\nu^*=\operatorname{argmax}_{0\le\nu\le(1-\mu)^{-1}}\mathrm E_F[\log(1-(X-\mu)\nu)]$." (The "log"
  before the expectation, present in (6), is missing in this display, as printed.) Proof: the dual
  at $\nu^*$ and Cramér's bound (17) of Proposition 9 for the empirical mean of
  $\log(1-(X_i-\mu)\nu^*)$.
- **Proposition 11.** "Fix any $F\in\mathcal A$ and $\mu<\mathrm E(F)$ and assume that the moment
  generating function $\mathrm E_F[\mathrm e^{\lambda X}]$ of $F$ exists in some neighborhood of
  $\lambda=0$. (i) For $\lambda_\mu=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}:\mathrm E_F[((1-X)/(1-\mu))^\lambda]\le1\}$,
  we have $\lambda_\mu>1$. (ii) For any $u\in\mathbb R$,
  $P_F[D_{\inf}(\hat F_t,\mu)\ge u,\ \hat\mu_t\le\mu]\le\mathrm e^{-t\Lambda^*(\mu)}$ if
  $u\le\Lambda^*(\mu)/\lambda_\mu$, and $\le2\mathrm e(1+\lambda_\mu t)\mathrm e^{-t\lambda_\mu u}$
  otherwise, where $\Lambda^*(x)=\sup_\lambda\{\lambda x-\log\mathrm E_F[\mathrm e^{\lambda X}]\}$ and we
  define $\lambda\mathrm e^{-\lambda}=0$ for $\lambda=+\infty$." Proof of (ii): a grid
  $\nu_{(m)}=(1+m/M_\delta)/(2(1-\mu))$, $M_\delta=\lceil1/(2\delta(1-\mu))\rceil$, concavity and the
  bounds $2(1-\mu)\ge L'(\nu_{(0)};\hat F_t,\mu)\ge-2(1-\mu)$ give (22); convexity of
  $\nu\mapsto\mathrm E_F[\mathrm e^{\lambda\log(1-(X-\mu)\nu)}]$ for $\lambda\ge1$ gives (23)–(24); then
  $\delta=1/(2t\lambda_\mu(1-\mu))$ and Cramér's bound (17).
- **Proposition 12.** "Fix any $u,\mu\in\mathbb R$ and $F\in\mathcal A$ such that $\mathrm E(F)<\mu<1$.
  Then $P_F[D_{\inf}(\hat F_t,\mu)\ge u]\le2\mathrm e(1+t)\exp(-t(u-\log\frac{1-\mathrm E(F)}{1-\mu}))$."
- **Lemma 13.** "For any $x>0$ and arm $i$, $\sum_{l=1}^\infty\mathbb 1[I^*(l)\le x,\ J(l)=i]\le\mathrm e^x$",
  with $I^*(l)\equiv\min_{i\in\{1,2,\cdots,K\}}I_i(l)$. Proof: $J(l)=i$ implies
  $I^*(l)=I_i(l)\ge\log T_i(l)$, and $\{J(l)=i,\ T_i(l)=t\}$ occurs for at most one $l$.
- **Lemma 14.** "It holds for any $\mu<\mu^*$ and arm $i$ that
  $\mathrm E[\sum_{l=1}^\infty\mathbb 1[\hat\mu^*(l)\le\mu,\ J(l)=i]]\le\inf_{j\in\mathcal I_{\mathrm{opt}}}\Big\{\frac{6\mathrm e}{(1-1/\lambda_{j,\mu})(1-\mathrm e^{-(1-1/\lambda_{j,\mu})\Lambda_j^*(\mu)})^3}\Big\}$."
  Its proof splits by the value $t$ of $T_j(l)$ ((25)) and applies Lemma 13 once for each $t$:
  (26) "$\sum_{l=1}^\infty\mathbb 1[\hat\mu^*(l)\le\mu,\ J(l)=i]\le\sum_{t=1}^\infty\mathbb 1[\hat\mu_{j,t}\le\mu]\,t\mathrm e^{tD_{\inf}(\hat F_{j,t},\mu)}$";
  then Proposition 11 for each term ((27)) and $\sum_tt\mathrm e^{-rt}\le(1-\mathrm e^{-r})^{-3}$,
  $\sum_tt^2\mathrm e^{-rt}\le2(1-\mathrm e^{-r})^{-3}$ ((28)).
- **Proof of Theorem 3.** (29): $T_i(n)$ is split into the rounds with $\hat\mu^*(l)\le\mu^*-\delta$
  (Lemma 14 with $\mu:=\mu^*-\delta$) and those with $\hat\mu^*(l)\ge\mu^*-\delta$; for the latter,
  "$I^*(l)\le\max_{i:\hat\mu_i(l)=\hat\mu^*(l)}I_i(l)=\max_{i:\hat\mu_i(l)=\hat\mu^*(l)}\log T_i(l)\le\log n$",
  then Lemma 7 and Proposition 10.
- **Lemma 15** (Appendix A, text layer only). For sufficiently small $\delta>0$, lower bounds of
  order $\delta^2$ on the rates of Theorem 3 (not transcribed; not used by Incognita).
- **§9 (summary).** The authors propose as future work to examine whether the moment generating
  function assumption can be weakened to the existence of moments $\mathrm E_{F_i}[X^m]$. They note
  that $D_{\inf}(\hat F_{i,t},\mu)$ only requires the moment $\mathrm E[(1-(X-\mu)\nu)^\lambda]$, that
  the MGF is used "only for the evaluation of $\hat\mu_{i,t}$", and that most evaluations involving
  $\hat\mu_{i,t}$ only need "$\sum_{t=1}^\infty t^p\Pr[|\hat\mu_{i,t}-\mu_i|>\delta]<\infty$ (30) for some
  $p\ge0$, which we can assure to hold only by assuming $\mathrm E_{F_i}[X^{2+p}]<\infty$ (Chow and
  Lai, 1975)". They conjecture that the MGF assumption can be weakened by using (30), and call it
  "an open problem".

## Techniques

- The dual (2) of $D_{\inf}$ on $\mathcal A$ (Theorem 2(ii)), proved by transporting the mass below
  $a$ to $a$ (Lemma 8) and the bounded-support result of Honda and Takemura (2010).
- Non-asymptotic large deviations for $D_{\inf}(\hat F_t,\mu)$: Cramér's bound at the dual maximizer
  for the lower deviations (Proposition 10, no moment needed beyond Lemma 6), and a grid of
  $O(1/\delta)$ dual variables with concavity for the upper deviations (Proposition 11(ii),
  (19)–(24)).
- The counting lemma for the minimum index (Lemma 13), applied once for each value of the optimal
  arm's count in Lemma 14 ((25)–(26)).
