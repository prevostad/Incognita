## Results

Copied from the text layer of the arXiv v1 PDF (2026-11-17). The words are the source's; the displays
were split over several lines by the extraction and are rebuilt in LaTeX with the source's symbols
(superscripts such as the class in $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}$ and the fraction bars were placed by
us from the layout). The source writes $\Re$ for the real line.

- **Standing setting (§2.1).** "We consider a bandit problem with K-arms indexed by $a\in[K]:=\{1,\dots,K\}$,
  with $K\ge2$. Let $\mathcal P(\Re)$ denote the collection of all probability measures on $\Re$. For
  $\nu\in\mathcal P(\Re)$, let $m(\nu)=\int_\Re x\,d\nu$ denote its mean. For $\mathcal M\subseteq\mathcal P(\Re)$, we
  denote a collection of bandit instances by $\mathcal M^K$, a collection of vectors of K distributions,
  each from $\mathcal M$." Each arm $a$ has "a reward distribution $\nu_a$ with expected reward
  $\mu_a=m(\nu_a)$", $\mu^*=\max\{\mu_a:a\in[K]\}$, $\Delta_a=\mu^*-\mu_a$. "Without loss of generality, we
  assume that arm 1 is the optimal arm, and means are ordered so that $\mu_1>\mu_2>\dots>\mu_K$ for the
  rest of the paper." $N_a(t)\triangleq\sum_{s=1}^t\mathbb I\{A_s=a\}$; for $N_a(t)\ge1$,
  $\hat\nu_a(t)=\frac1{N_a(t)}\sum_{s=1}^t\delta_{Y_s}\mathbb I\{A_s=a\}$;
  $\mathbb E[R(T)]\triangleq\mathbb E\big[T\mu_1-\sum_{t=1}^TY_t\big]=\sum_{a=1}^K\Delta_a\mathbb E[N_a(T)]$, the
  expectation under $\mathbb P^\pi_\nu$ (measure spaces as in LS, Chapter 4).
- **Definition 2.1.** "An algorithm is said to be $\mathcal M^K$-optimal if for every $\nu\in\mathcal M^K$ and for
  each sub-optimal arm $a$, the following holds:
  $$\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E[N_a(T)]}{\log T}=\frac1{\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal M}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)},\qquad(1)$$
  where $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal M}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1):=\inf\{\mathrm{KL}(\nu_a,\nu'):\nu'\in\mathcal M\text{ and }\mathbb E[\nu']\ge\mu_1\}$
  and $\mathrm{KL}(\nu_a,\nu')=\int\log\frac{d\nu_a}{d\nu'}d\nu_a$ is the Kullback-Leibler divergence between
  $\nu_a$ and $\nu'$."
- **$\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ at a level (after Definition 2.1).** "More generally, for a probability distribution $\nu$
  and $x\in\Re$, $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal M}_{\inf}(\nu,x):=\inf\{\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\nu'):\nu'\in\mathcal M\text{ and }\mathbb E[\nu']\ge x\}$."
  (A **non-strict** inequality on the mean.)
- **Remark 2.2.** "We use $\mathcal M$ to denote the general class of distributions. When $\mathcal M$ satisfies
  Assumptions 2.3 and 2.4, we denote the corresponding subclass by $\mathcal L$. For notational convenience,
  we drop the superscript in $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\cdot,\cdot)$ when the underlying class is clear from context."
- **The class $\mathcal L$ (§2.2, (2)).** "we restrict our attention to subclasses $\mathcal L$ that satisfy the
  following property: $\nu\in\mathcal L\implies\nu$ satisfies Assumptions 2.3 and 2.4. (2)" (The
  $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ inside the assumptions is taken over $\mathcal L$ itself.)
- **Assumption 2.3.** "For $\nu\in\mathcal L$, let $\hat\nu_n$ be the empirical distribution of $n$ i.i.d.
  samples from $\nu$, having mean $m(\nu)$. Let $g(n)=C_1\log(1+n)+C_2$ for some $C_1,C_2>0$. Then, $\nu$,
  $\mathcal L$ satisfy:
  $$\mathbb P\Big(\exists n\in\mathbb N:\ n\,\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\hat\nu_n,m(\nu))-g(n)\ge x\Big)\le e^{-x}.\qquad(3)"$$
  The statement has no quantifier on $x$ (checked in both extractions). In the proof of Lemma A.2 it
  is applied at $x=\log(K+r-1)+2\log\log(K+r-1)$, $r\ge2$, which is positive.
- **Assumption 2.4.** "For $\nu\in\mathcal L$, let $\hat\nu_n$ be the empirical distribution of $n$ i.i.d.
  samples from $\nu$, having mean $m(\nu)$, and let $\delta>0$. Then there exist constants $d_0>0$ and
  $c_\nu>0$ s.t. for all $d<d_0$ the following holds:
  $$\mathbb P\Big(\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\hat\nu_n,m(\nu)+\delta)\le\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\nu,m(\nu)+\delta)-d\Big)\le e^{-nc_\nu d^2}.\qquad(4)"$$
  No quantifier on $n$ is printed; $d_0$ and $c_\nu$ are not said to depend on $\delta$, though they come
  after it.
- **After the assumptions (§2.2).** If $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\cdot,x)$ is "appropriately continuous", the
  empirical values converge to $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\nu,m(\nu))$ and to $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}(\nu,x)>0$ for
  $x>m(\nu)$; the two assumptions "correspond to an exponential concentration of the empirical
  $\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal L}_{\inf}$ around these limits". Bounded supports (Honda and Takemura 2010) and a known
  bound on the $(1+\epsilon)$th moment (AJK21) are given as examples (Appendix A.1).
- **Algorithm 1, "Generalized KLinf-UCB$(K,\{f_a(\cdot)\}_{a=1}^K)$".** "Input: $K$; $\mathcal L$; exploration
  functions for each arm, i.e., $f_a(\cdot)$. Initialization: Pull each arm $a\in[K]$ once. Set
  $t\leftarrow K+1$. Compute $\hat\nu_a(t)$, and update $N_a(t)$ for all arms $a\in[K]$." Then, for
  $t=K+1,K+2,\dots$: for each arm, "Compute index
  $U_a(N_a(t),t)=\sup\big\{\mathbb E(\nu):\nu\in\mathcal L,\ \mathrm{KL}(\hat\nu_a(t),\nu)\le\frac{f_a(t)}{N_a(t)}\big\}$";
  "Pick an arm $A_{t+1}\in\arg\max_{a\in[K]}U_a(N_a(t),t)$"; "Set $t\leftarrow t+1$"; update $\hat\nu_a(t)$
  and $N_a(t)$. The text after it defines the index equivalently as
  $U_a(N_a(t),t)=\sup\big\{x\in\Re:\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\hat\nu_a(t),x)\le\frac{f_a(t)}{N_a(t)}\big\}$, "monotonically
  increasing in the second argument when the first argument is fixed".
- **Paragraph before Theorem 3.1.** The batched variant of AJK21 is set aside: "We instead focus on the
  unit-batch version of the algorithm and establish its asymptotic optimality over $\mathcal L$."
- **Theorem 3.1.** "For $\nu\in\mathcal L^K$ and for every suboptimal arm $a$, let
  $f_a(t)=\log(t)+2\log\log(t)+g(N_a(t))$. Then Generalized KLinf-UCB, with inputs $(K,f_a(.))$ satisfies
  $$\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E[N_a(T)]}{\log(T)}\le\frac1{\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)}."$$
  Hypotheses in force: the standing setting of §2.1 ($K\ge2$; arm 1 optimal and
  $\mu_1>\mu_2>\dots>\mu_K$), $\mathcal L$ as in (2), $g$ from Assumption 2.3. "The proof of the above
  theorem closely follows that of Agrawal et al. (2021a, Theorem 1) and is presented in the Appendix A.2
  for completeness." (Agrawal et al. 2021a is AJK21.)
- **Appendix A.1, examples.** "Moment bounded distributions: For $\varepsilon>0$ and $B>0$, define class
  $\mathcal L_{B,\varepsilon}$ as: $\mathcal L_{B,\varepsilon}\triangleq\{\nu\in\mathcal P(\Re):\mathbb E_\nu\big[|X|^{1+\varepsilon}\big]\le B\}$. Every element
  $\nu\in\mathcal L_{B,\varepsilon}$ satisfies Assumptions 2.3 and 2.4 with $g(n)=2\log(1+n)+1$ (see, Agrawal et al.
  (2021a, Proposition 5) and Agrawal et al. (2021a, Lemma 6) for details)." "Bounded support
  distributions: For constants $a,b\in\Re$, define class $\mathcal L_{[a,b]}$ as:
  $\mathcal L_{[a,b]}\triangleq\{\nu\in\mathcal P(\Re):\mathrm{Supp}(\nu)\subseteq[a,b]\}$. This distribution class also
  satisfies the above assumptions with $g(n)=\log(1+n)+1$." Its proof is only sketched (along AJK21,
  with the dual of HT10, §4, and "Agrawal et al. (2021b, Lemma F.1)"). Semi-bounded laws and laws with
  a finite mean only are **not** treated.
- **Lemma A.1.** "For $T\ge K+1$, and $d>0$,
  $\sum_{j=1}^TE_{1j}\le\Big(\frac{\log(T)}{\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)-d}+O(\log\log(T))\Big)$." (Stated "from Agrawal et
  al. (2021a)", exact form of the $O(\log\log T)$ term referred to the proof of AJK21, Lemma 14.)
- **Lemma A.2.** "For $T>K$, $\mathbb E(D_T)\le\frac1{\log(K+1)}$." (AJK21, Lemma 18, restated; proved from
  Assumption 2.3 through (13).)

## Techniques

- Proof of Theorem 3.1 (Appendix A.2, pp. 10–12, read in full on 2026-11-17): the event $\{A_t=a\}$ is
  contained in the union (11) of an underestimation of the optimal arm's index ($U_1\le\mu_1$, term
  $D_T$) and an overestimation of the index of $a$ ($U_a>\mu_1$, term $E_T$);
  $\mathbb E[N_a(T)]\le1+\mathbb E[D_T]+\mathbb E[E_T]$. With $0<d\le\min_{a>1}\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)$, $E_T$ is
  split into $E_{1t}$ (empirical $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ above $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)-d$: a deterministic count,
  Lemma A.1) and $E_{2t}$ (empirical $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ below it: Assumption 2.4 "with $\delta=\mu_1-\mu_a$",
  summed to at most $1+1/(c_{\nu_a}d^2)$); $D_T$ by Lemma A.2 (Assumption 2.3 at the sampling times of
  the arm); (14) is then optimised over $d$, $d=\big(c'_{\nu_a}(\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1))^2/\log T\big)^{1/3}$, with $c'_{\nu_a}=2o(1)/c_{\nu_a}$ (p. 12, after (14)).
  No compactness or bounded support is used.
- The proof follows AJK21, Theorem 1, "specialized to batches of size one per trial".
- Kurtosis and centred moments are not mentioned anywhere in the text (search of the PDF text layer,
  P005).
- *Remarks (not in the source).* (a) In App. A.2 the lower deviation term is bounded by
  $\sum_te^{-tc_{\nu_a}d^2}$, with the round $t$ in place of the number $N_a(t)$ of samples of arm $a$ at
  which (4) applies (observed in the independent check of the P005 report; confirmed on 2026-11-17 in
  the display after Lemma A.1); P005 counts over the sample sizes instead. (b) In the same proof the
  sums $\sum_{r=2}^T$ of Lemma A.2 are indexed by the pull count $r$ of arm $a$ while the indices are those
  of arm 1 at the times $T_{ar}$; the display writes $T_{kr}$ once for $T_{ar}$.
