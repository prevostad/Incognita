## Results

Displays as returned by the fetch tool (macros normalised by it); the surrounding words are a
paraphrase, not a quotation.

- **Definition (§2.1).** For a class $\mathcal M$ of laws, a law $\nu$ and $x\in\mathbb R$,
  $$\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal{M}}_{\inf}(\nu,x):=\inf\left\{\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\nu'):\ \nu'\in\mathcal{M}\text{ and }\mathbb{E}[\nu']\ge x\right\}$$
  (a **non-strict** inequality on the mean).
- **Assumption 2.3 (§2.2, (3)).** With $g(n)=C_1\log(1+n)+C_2$ for constants $C_1,C_2>0$, for a law
  $\nu\in\mathcal L$, $\hat\nu_n$ the empirical law of $n$ i.i.d. samples of $\nu$ and $x$ (no
  explicit "for all $x$" in the text, per the tool):
  $$\mathbb{P}\left(\exists n\in\mathbb{N}:\ n\,\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\inf}(\hat{\nu}_n,m(\nu))-g(n)\ge x\right)\le e^{-x}.$$
- **Assumption 2.4 (§2.2, (4)).** For every $\delta>0$ there are $d_0>0$ and $c_\nu>0$ such that for
  every $d<d_0$ (and every $n\ge1$)
  $$\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\inf}(\hat{\nu}_n,m(\nu)+\delta)\le\mathrm{KL}^{\mathcal{L}}_{\inf}(\nu,m(\nu)+\delta)-d\right)\le e^{-n c_\nu d^2}.$$
- **The class $\mathcal L$ (§2.2):** the laws satisfying Assumptions 2.3 and 2.4 (the
  $\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}$ inside them is taken over $\mathcal L$ itself).
- **KLinf-UCB (§3).** Each arm is pulled once, then the arm with the largest index
  $$U_a(N_a(t),t)=\sup\left\{x\in\mathbb R:\ \mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\hat{\nu}_a(t),x)\le\frac{f_a(t)}{N_a(t)}\right\}.$$
- **Theorem 3.1.** For an instance $\boldsymbol\nu\in\mathcal L^K$ and a suboptimal arm $a$,
  KLinf-UCB with $f_a(t)=\log(t)+2\log\log(t)+g(N_a(t))$ satisfies
  $$\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[N_a(T)]}{\log(T)}\le\frac{1}{\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)}.$$
- **Examples (Appendix A.1, per the tool):** the laws with $\mathbb E|X|^{1+\varepsilon}\le B$
  (Assumptions 2.3–2.4 with $g(n)=2\log(1+n)+1$, from AJK21, Proposition 5 and Lemma 6) and the laws
  on $[a,b]$ ($g(n)=\log(1+n)+1$, a sketch through the dual of HT10). Semi-bounded laws and laws with
  a finite mean only are **not** treated.

## Techniques

- Proof of Theorem 3.1 (Appendix A.2, outline per the tool): the pulls of a suboptimal arm $a$ are
  split into rounds where the optimal arm's index is at most $\mu_1$ (bounded through Assumption
  2.3, Lemma A.2 = AJK21, Lemma 18) and rounds where the index of $a$ exceeds $\mu_1$; the latter
  are split with a slack $d$ into a deterministic count (Lemma A.1, from AJK21, Lemma 14: at most
  $\log T/(\mathrm{KL}_{\inf}(\nu_a,\mu_1)-d)+O(\log\log T)$) and deviation rounds bounded by
  Assumption 2.4 at the level $\mu_1$ (sum $\le1+1/(c_{\nu_a}d^2)$). No compactness or bounded
  support is used in A.2.
- The proof follows AJK21, Theorem 1.
- Kurtosis and centred moments are not mentioned anywhere in the text (search of the PDF text layer,
  P005).
- *Remark (not in the source, from the independent check of the P005 report):* in App. A.2 the lower
  deviation term is bounded by $\sum_te^{-tc_{\nu_a}d^2}$, with the round $t$ in place of the number of samples
  of arm $a$; P005 counts over the sample sizes instead.
