## Results

Mathematics is transcribed into LaTeX from the PDF text.

- **Setting, Section 1, p. 1.** "To set the notation, let $K\ge2$ be the number of actions (or arms) and
  $n\ge K$ be the time horizon." At each $t$, the agent chooses $p_t$, "the environment chooses a
  reward vector $g_t=(g_{1,t},\dots,g_{K,t})\in[0,1]^K$, and simultaneously (independently), the agent
  draws the action (or arm) $I_t$ according to the distribution $p_t$, (iii) the agent only gets to see
  his own reward $g_{I_t,t}$." Stochastic case: "parameterized by a $K$-tuple of probability
  distributions $(\nu_1,\dots,\nu_K)$ on $[0,1]$"; adversarial case: the environment "may choose a
  reward vector $g_t$ as a function of the past decisions $I_1,\dots,I_{t-1}$ (and possibly of an
  independent randomization)". "A policy [...] is a function that maps any history to a probability
  distribution on $\{1,\dots,K\}$." Regret: "$R_n=\max_{i=1,\dots,K}\mathbb E\sum_{t=1}^n(g_{i,t}-g_{I_t,t})$."
- **The sentence on Auer et al., Section 1, p. 1, verbatim.** "The minimax rate of the expected regret
  is $\inf\sup R_n$, where the infimum is taken over all policies and the supremum over all $K$-tuples
  of probability distributions on $[0,1]$ for the stochastic case and over all adversarial
  environments with rewards in $[0,1]$ for the adversarial case. Auer et al. (1995) proved an upper
  bound on this quantity in the adversarial case (and thus also in the stochastic case) and a lower
  bound in the stochastic case (and thus also in the adversarial case). We recall here the results of
  Auer et al. (1995, 2003)."
  - "Auer et al. (1995)" is, in the reference list, "P. Auer, N. Cesa-Bianchi, Y. Freund, and R.
    Schapire. Gambling in a rigged casino: the adversarial multi-armed bandit problem. In Proceedings
    of the 36th Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science, pages 322–331. IEEE Computer
    Society Press, 1995."; "Auer et al. (2003)" is "The non-stochastic multi-armed bandit problem.
    SIAM Journal on Computing, 32(1):48–77, 2003" (dated 2003 here; ACFS02 in our bibliography).
  - The paper gives no argument for "(and thus also ...)" beyond this sentence. The same phrase
    recurs before Table 1, p. 4: "The bounds for MOSS and UCB1 hold in the stochastic setting only.
    The bounds for INF and EXP3 hold in the adversarial setting (and thus also in the stochastic
    case)."
- **Footnote 1, p. 1 (on the regret definition).** "In the case of an oblivious deterministic opponent
  this regret is equal to the strong regret: $\mathbb E\max_i\sum_{t=1}^n(g_{i,t}-g_{I_t,t})$. In the case of
  an oblivious stochastic opponent (parametrized by $nK$ probability distributions over $[0,1]$), one
  can prove that the difference between the regret and the strong regret is at most $\sqrt{n\log K}$."
- **Theorem 1, p. 1–2 (recalled from Auer et al.).** "The EXP3 policy described in Fig.1 satisfies
  $\sup R_n\le2.7\sqrt{nK\log K}$, for $\eta=\min\bigl(0.8\sqrt{\frac{\log K}{nK}},\frac1K\bigr)$, where the
  supremum is taken over all adversarial environments. Besides we have $\inf\sup R_n\ge\frac1{20}\sqrt{nK}$,
  where the supremum is taken over all $K$-tuple of probability distributions on $[0,1]$."
- **Theorem 2, p. 2 (recalled).** UCB1: $R_n\le10\bigl(\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}\frac1{\Delta_i}\bigr)\log n$ (1);
  the Lai–Robbins lower bound (2), credited to "(Lai and Robbins, 1985, Theorem 1) applied to the
  parametric family of Bernoulli distributions".
- **Theorem 4 (INF), Section 2, p. 3, verbatim.** "For any real $q>1$, the Implicitly Normalized
  Forecaster with $\psi(x)=\frac1K\bigl(\frac{9\sqrt{qnK}}{-x}\bigr)^q+\frac{q^{q/(2q-2)}}{\sqrt{nK}}$ satisfies
  $$\sup R_n\le\frac{37}{1-1/q}\sqrt{qnK},\quad(6)$$
  where the supremum is taken over all adversarial environments with rewards in $[0,1]$." After it:
  "For $q=3$, inequality (6) gives $\sup R_n\le100\sqrt{nK}$." For
  $\psi(x)=\frac1K\bigl(\frac{2.5\sqrt{nK}}{-x}\bigr)^{1.5}$, "we have shown that $\sup R_n\le15\sqrt{nK}$" (with
  "proof omitted here").
- **Remark 2, p. 3.** The policies "are not 'anytime' since their implementation requires the knowledge
  of the horizon $n$" (doubling trick, citing Section 2.3 of Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi 2006).
- **MOSS, Figure 3, p. 3.** Index $B_{i,s}=\widehat X_{i,s}+\sqrt{\frac{\max(\log(\frac n{Ks}),0)}s}$ for
  $s\ge1$, $B_{i,0}=+\infty$; "At time $t$, draw an arm maximizing $B_{i,T_i(t-1)}$."
- **Theorem 5 (MOSS), Section 3, p. 3, verbatim.** "MOSS satisfies
  $$\sup R_n\le49\sqrt{nK},\quad(7)$$
  where the supremum is taken over all $K$-tuple of probability distributions on $[0,1]$." The proof
  (Appendix C) ends with $R_n\le48.3\sqrt{nK}+K$ and a case split on $K\ge n/49^2$.
- **Remark 3, p. 4.** "A careful tuning of the constants in front and inside the logarithmic term of
  $B_{i,s}$ and of the thresholds used in the proof leads to $\sup R_n\le5.7\sqrt{nK}$. However, it makes
  the proof more intricate. So we will only prove (7)."
- **Theorem 6, p. 4.** "MOSS satisfies $R_n\le23K\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}\frac{\max\left(\log\left(\frac{n\Delta_i^2}K\right),1\right)}{\Delta_i}$. (8)"

## Techniques

INF: probabilities $p_{i,t+1}=\psi(\tilde G_{i,t}-C_t)$ with an implicit normalisation $C_t$ (Lemma 3,
Fig. 2); EXP3 is the case $\psi(u)=\eta+\exp(\eta u)$ (Remark 1). MOSS: UCB index with exploration
$\log(n/(Ks))$ truncated at 0; proof by decoupling arms via thresholds $z_i=\mu_1-\Delta_i/2$, Hoeffding,
and peeling with Hoeffding's maximal inequality (sketch after Theorem 5, Appendix C). The paper cites
Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi (2006) only for Sections 2.1 and 2.3 of the book, not for Theorem 6.11.
