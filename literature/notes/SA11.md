### Setting (§2, p. 3)

"In the stochastic multi-armed bandit problem with $K\ge2$ arms, at each time step $t=1,2,\ldots$, an
agent has to choose an arm $I_t$ in the set $\{1,\ldots,K\}$ and obtains a reward drawn from $\nu_{I_t}$
independently from the past (actions and observations). The environment is thus parameterized by a
$K$-tuple of probability distributions $\theta=(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K)$. The agent aims at maximizing his
rewards. He does not know $\theta$ but knows that it belongs to some set $\Theta$. We assume for simplicity
that $\Theta\subset\bar\Theta$, where $\bar\Theta$ denotes the set of all $K$-tuple of probability distributions on
$[0,1]$. We thus assume that the rewards are in $[0,1]$.

For each arm $k$ and all times $t\ge1$, let $T_k(t)=\sum_{s=1}^t\mathbb 1_{I_s=k}$ denote the number of times
arm $k$ was pulled from round $1$ to round $t$, and $X_{k,1},X_{k,2},\ldots,X_{k,T_k(t)}$ the sequence of
associated rewards. For an environment parameterized by $\theta=(\nu_1,\ldots,\nu_K)$, let $\mathbb P_\theta$
denote the distribution on the probability space such that for any $k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$, the random
variables $X_{k,1},X_{k,2},\ldots$ are i.i.d. realizations of $\nu_k$, and such that these $K$ infinite
sequence of random variables are independent. Let $\mathbb E_\theta$ denote the associated expectation.

Let $\mu_k=\int x\,d\nu_k(x)$ be the mean reward of arm $k$. Introduce $\mu^*=\max_k\mu_k$ and fix an arm
$k^*\in\operatorname{argmax}_{k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}\mu_k$, that is, $k^*$ has the best expected reward. The
suboptimality of arm $k$ is measured by $\Delta_k=\mu^*-\mu_k$. The agent aims at minimizing its regret
defined as the difference between the cumulative reward he would have got by always drawing the
best arm and the cumulative reward he actually got. At time $n\ge1$, its regret is thus
$$\hat R_n=\sum_{t=1}^nX_{k^*,t}-\sum_{t=1}^nX_{I_t,T_{I_t}(t)}.\qquad(1)$$"

Then $\mathbb E_\theta\hat R_n=\sum_{k=1}^K\Delta_k\mathbb E_\theta[T_k(n)]$, and: "Other notions of regret exists in
the literature: the quantity $\sum_{k=1}^K\Delta_kT_k(n)$ is called the pseudo regret and may be more
practical to study, and the quantity $\max_k\sum_{t=1}^nX_{k,t}-\sum_{t=1}^nX_{I_t,T_{I_t}(t)}$ defines the regret
in adversarial settings. Results and ideas we want to convey here are more suited to definition (1),
and taking another definition of the regret would only bring some more technical intricacies."

So $\hat R_n$ is the **reward regret against the reward sequence of the fixed arm $k^*$** (reward-table
model), not the pseudo-regret. "Policy" is not defined formally.

### Definition 1 (p. 4)

Introduced by: "[...] We thus define below the notion of being $f$-upper tailed for both quantities.
Define $\mathbb R_+^*=\{x\in\mathbb R:x>0\}$, and let $\Delta=\min_{k\neq k^*}\Delta_k$ be the gap between the best
arm and second best arm."

"**Definition 1** ($f$-$\mathcal T$ and $f$-$\mathcal R$). *Consider a mapping $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R_+^*$. A policy has
$f$-upper tailed sampling Times (in short, we will say that the policy is $f$-$\mathcal T$) if and only if*
$$\exists C,\tilde C>0,\ \forall\theta\in\Theta\text{ such that }\Delta\neq0,\qquad
\forall n\ge2,\ \forall k\neq k^*,\ \mathbb P_\theta\Big(T_k(n)\ge C\frac{\log n}{\Delta_k^2}\Big)\le\frac{\tilde C}{f(n)}.$$
*A policy has $f$-upper tailed Regret (in short, $f$-$\mathcal R$) if and only if*
$$\exists C,\tilde C>0,\ \forall\theta\in\Theta\text{ such that }\Delta\neq0,\ \forall n\ge2,\
\mathbb P_\theta\Big(\hat R_n\ge C\frac{\log n}{\Delta}\Big)\le\frac{\tilde C}{f(n)}."$$

After it: "We will sometimes prefer to denote $f(n)$-$\mathcal T$ (resp. $f(n)$-$\mathcal R$) instead of
$f$-$\mathcal T$ (resp. $f$-$\mathcal R$) for readability. Note also that, for sake of simplicity, we leave
aside the degenerated case of $\Delta$ being null (i.e. when there are at least two optimal arms).

In this definition, we considered that the number $K$ of arms is fixed, meaning that $C$ and $\tilde C$
may depend on $K$. The thresholds considered on $T_k(n)$ and $\hat R_n$ directly come from known tight
upper bounds on the expectation of these quantities for several policies."

UCB1 (2): each arm once, then for $t\ge K+1$,
$I_t\in\operatorname{argmax}_{k}\big\{\hat X_{k,T_k(t-1)}+\sqrt{2\log t/T_k(t-1)}\big\}$, with
$\hat X_{k,s}=\frac1s\sum_{t=1}^sX_{k,t}$. p. 5: "For this policy, Auer et al. [3] proved:
$\forall n\ge3,\ \mathbb E[T_k(n)]\le12\frac{\log n}{\Delta_k^2}$ and
$\mathbb E_\theta\hat R_n\le12\sum_{k=1}^K\frac{\log n}{\Delta_k}\le12K\frac{\log n}{\Delta}$." (The sum over all $k$ is
as printed; $k=k^*$ would give $\Delta_{k^*}=0$.) "Audibert et al. [2] proved that UCB1 is
$\log^3$-$\mathcal T$ and $\log^3$-$\mathcal R$ where $\log^3$ is the function $x\mapsto[\log(x)]^3$." With
$\rho\log t$ in place of $2\log t$ ($\rho>1/2$): $\log^{2\rho-1}$-$\mathcal T$ and $\log^{2\rho-1}$-$\mathcal R$
(credited to [2]). UCB-H replaces $2\log t$ by $2\log n$; "Audibert et al. showed that UCB-H is
$n$-$\mathcal T$ and $n$-$\mathcal R$ (as it will be recalled in Theorem 3.5)".

### Definition 2 (p. 5)

"[...] whether a policy knows in advance the horizon $n$ or not matters a lot, that is why we
introduce the following terms.

**Definition 2.** *A policy that uses the knowledge of the horizon $n$ (e.g. UCB-H) is a horizon
policy. A policy that does not use the knowledge of $n$ (e.g. UCB1) is an anytime policy.*"

How Definition 1 applies to a horizon policy is not said; Theorem 3.5 ("UCB-H is $n^\beta$-$\mathcal R$")
uses UCB-H "for a given $n$", i.e. the policy for horizon $n$ evaluated at time $n$ (interpretation).

### Definition 3 (p. 5)

"We now introduce the weak notion of $f$-upper tailed as this notion will be used to get our
strongest impossibility results.

**Definition 3** ($f$-w$\mathcal T$ and $f$-w$\mathcal R$). *Consider a mapping $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R_+^*$. A policy
has weak $f$-upper tailed sampling Times (in short, we will say that the policy is $f$-w$\mathcal T$) if
and only if*
$$\forall\theta\in\Theta\text{ such that }\Delta\neq0,\qquad
\exists C,\tilde C>0,\ \forall n\ge2,\ \forall k\neq k^*,\ \mathbb P_\theta\Big(T_k(n)\ge C\frac{\log n}{\Delta_k^2}\Big)\le\frac{\tilde C}{f(n)}.$$
*A policy has weak $f$-upper tailed Regret (in short, $f$-w$\mathcal R$) if and only if*
$$\forall\theta\in\Theta\text{ such that }\Delta\neq0,\ \exists C,\tilde C>0,\ \forall n\ge2,\
\mathbb P_\theta\Big(\hat R_n\ge C\frac{\log n}{\Delta}\Big)\le\frac{\tilde C}{f(n)}."$$

"The only difference between $f$-$\mathcal T$ and $f$-w$\mathcal T$ (and between $f$-$\mathcal R$ and $f$-w$\mathcal R$) is the
interchange of "$\forall\theta$" and "$\exists C,\tilde C$". Consequently, a policy that is $f$-$\mathcal T$
(respectively $f$-$\mathcal R$) is $f$-w$\mathcal T$ (respectively $f$-w$\mathcal R$). Let us detail the links between
the $f$-$\mathcal T$, $f$-$\mathcal R$, $f$-w$\mathcal T$ and $f$-w$\mathcal R$."

### Proposition 2.1 (p. 6)

"**Proposition 2.1.** *Assume that there exists $\alpha,\beta>0$ such that $f(n)\le\alpha n^\beta$ for any
$n\ge2$. We have*
$$f\text{-}\mathcal T\Rightarrow f\text{-}\mathcal R\Rightarrow f\text{-w}\mathcal R\Leftrightarrow f\text{-w}\mathcal T.$$
The proof of this proposition is technical but rather straightforward. Note that we do not have
$f$-$\mathcal R\Rightarrow f$-$\mathcal T$, because the agent may not regret having pulled a suboptimal arm if the
latter has delivered good rewards. Note also that $f$ is required to be at most polynomial: if not
some rare events such as unlikely deviations of rewards towards their actual mean can not be
neglected, and none of the implications hold in general (except, of course,
$f$-$\mathcal R\Rightarrow f$-w$\mathcal R$ and $f$-$\mathcal T\Rightarrow f$-w$\mathcal T$)." The proof (§7) was not read.

### §3 (pp. 6–9)

- §3 opening: "Here and in section 4 we mostly deal with anytime policies, and the word policy (or
  algorithm) implicitly refers to anytime policy."
- Notation (p. 8): "we find it more convenient to denote $f\gg_{+\infty}g$ rather than the usual
  notation $g=o(f)$, which has the following meaning: $\forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists N\ge0,\ \forall n\ge N,\ g(n)\le\varepsilon f(n)$."
- Theorem 3.1 (Lebesgue–Radon–Nikodym) with the convention $\frac{d\mu_1}{d\mu_2}=+\infty$ off the
  support of $\mu_2$; Lemma 3.2 ($\mu_1(\frac{d\mu_1}{d\mu_2}=0)=0$;
  $\mu_2(\frac{d\mu_1}{d\mu_2}>0)>0\Leftrightarrow\mu_1(\frac{d\mu_2}{d\mu_1}>0)>0$).
- **Theorem 3.3.** "*Let $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R_+^*$ be greater than order $\log^\alpha$, that is for any
  $\alpha>0$, $f\gg_{+\infty}\log^\alpha$. Assume that there exists $\theta,\tilde\theta\in\Theta$, and
  $k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}$ such that: (a) $\nu_k=\tilde\nu_k$, (b) $k$ is the index of the best arm in $\theta$ but
  not in $\tilde\theta$, (c) $\forall\ell\neq k$, $\mathbb P_{\tilde\theta}\big(\frac{d\nu_\ell}{d\tilde\nu_\ell}(X_{\ell,1})>0\big)>0$. Then there is
  no $f$-w$\mathcal T$ anytime policy, and hence no $f$-$\mathcal R$ anytime policy.*" Example after it: $K=2$,
  $\Theta=\{(\mathrm{Ber}(\frac14),\delta_{\frac12}),(\mathrm{Ber}(\frac34),\delta_{\frac12})\}$ satisfies the three conditions.
- **Corollary 3.4.** "*If $\Theta$ is the whole set $\bar\Theta$ of all $K$-tuples of measures on $[0,1]$, then
  there is no $f$-$\mathcal R$ anytime policy, where $f$ is any function such that $f\gg_{+\infty}\log^\alpha$ for
  all $\alpha>0$.*"
- UCB-H: "for a given $n$, plays at time $t\ge K+1$,
  $I_t\in\operatorname{argmax}_k\{\hat X_{k,T_k(t-1)}+\sqrt{2\log n/T_k(t-1)}\}$". **Theorem 3.5.** "*For any
  $\beta>0$, UCB-H is $n^\beta$-$\mathcal R$.*" UCB-H($\rho$) (3) with $\rho\log n$, $\rho>1$, extends it.
- UCB1($\rho$) (4): each arm once, then $I_t\in\operatorname{argmax}_k\{\hat X_{k,T_k(t-1)}+\sqrt{\rho\log t/T_k(t-1)}\}$.
  **Theorem 3.6.** "*For any $\rho>1/2$, UCB1($\rho$) is $\log^{2\rho-1}$-$\mathcal R$.*"

## Techniques

- Impossibility for anytime policies (Theorem 3.3, sketch p. 8, full proof §7 not read): a policy
  that is $f$-w$\mathcal T$ concentrates its pulls on arm $k$ in $\theta$; at a time $N$ of order $\log n$, an
  event where $k$ is pulled $\approx N$ times and the other arms "behave as if in $\theta$" has
  $\mathbb P_{\tilde\theta}$-probability at least $(\eta a)^T$ with $T=\sum_{\ell\neq k}T_\ell(N)$ of order $\log N$, by a
  lower bound $\eta^T$ on the likelihood ratio $d\mathbb P_\theta/d\mathbb P_{\tilde\theta}$; condition (c) is the
  formalisation of that last step through the Radon–Nikodym decomposition (Theorem 3.1, Lemma 3.2,
  credited to [22], p. 121).

Differences with the roadmap's transcriptions (2026-11-17):
- The source's $\hat R_n$ (1) is the reward regret against the rewards $X_{k^*,1},\ldots,X_{k^*,n}$ of the
  fixed best arm $k^*$, which the source distinguishes from the pseudo-regret $\sum_k\Delta_kT_k(n)$. The
  roadmap pins $\hat R_T:=\sum_{t<T}\Delta_{A_t}$ (pseudo-regret, LML's `regret`), and K33 writes $\hat R_n$
  without saying which: a `HasUpperTailedRegret` built on LML's `regret` is a different predicate
  from $f$-$\mathcal R$ (the remark after Proposition 2.1, that $f$-$\mathcal R\not\Rightarrow f$-$\mathcal T$, is about
  the reward regret).
- The class: the source quantifies over $\theta\in\Theta$ with $\Theta\subset\bar\Theta$ (laws on $[0,1]$), and only
  over $\theta$ with $\Delta\neq0$ (a unique optimal arm), $\Delta=\min_{k\neq k^*}\Delta_k$; K33 says "every
  environment with $\Delta\neq0$" without the class and without defining $\Delta$.
- Counts: "$\forall k\neq k^*$"; $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R_+^*$ (positive; Theorem 3.3 takes $f:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R_+^*$);
  $C,\tilde C>0$ may depend on $K$; $n\ge2$; $T_k(n)$ counts rounds $1,\ldots,n$ (same value as the
  roadmap's $N_k(n)$). K33 omits $\forall k\neq k^*$ and the range of $f$.
- K33 states Theorem 3.3 for "$f$-upper tailed regret" (strong); the source excludes $f$-w$\mathcal T$
  anytime policies, hence $f$-$\mathcal R$ (and, by Proposition 2.1 when $f$ is at most polynomial,
  $f$-w$\mathcal R$). K33 is weaker than the source, not wrong. The roadmap's row cites only
  Definition 1; the weak Definition 3 is not in the table.
- Horizon policies: Definition 1 does not say how a horizon policy is evaluated; the roadmap's
  family versions (policy $\pi_n$ at time $n$) are an interpretation consistent with Theorem 3.5.
