## Results

Numbering is the ar5iv rendering's (arXiv). The finite-armed bandit theorem is **Theorem 6.1**
there; a citation "Theorem 6" must be checked against the PMLR PDF (not read).

- **Setting, Section 1.** "A finite-action partial monitoring game is described by two functions, the
  loss function $\mathcal L:[k]\times\mathcal X\to[0,1]$ and a signal function $\Phi$ [...], where
  $[k]=\{1,2,\dots,k\}$ and $\mathcal X$ and $\Sigma$ are topological spaces." "At the start of the game
  the adversary secretly chooses a sequence of outcomes" $x_1,\dots,x_n\in\mathcal X$; in round $t$,
  after the learner chooses $A_t$ they suffer the loss $\mathcal L(A_t,x_t)$ and observe only the signal
  $\Phi(A_t,x_t)$. "The learner knows $\mathcal L$, $\Phi$ and $n$ and sequentially chooses actions".
  "Note, this is the oblivious setting because the adversary chooses all the losses at the start of
  the game." "A policy $\pi$ is a function mapping action/signal sequences to probability distributions
  over actions" (the learner may randomise). Regret:
  $$\mathfrak R_n(\pi,x)=\max_{a\in[k]}\mathbb E\Big[\sum_{t=1}^n\mathcal L(A_t,x_t)-\mathcal L(a,x_t)\Big],$$
  "where the expectation is taken with respect to the randomness in the learner's choices which
  follow $\pi$." Minimax regret:
  $$\mathfrak R_n^*=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X^n}\mathfrak R_n(\pi,x),$$
  "where $\Pi$ is the space of all policies." Bayesian regret: "The Bayesian regret of a policy $\pi$
  with respect to prior $\nu\in\mathscr Q$ is"
  $$\mathfrak{BR}_n(\pi,\nu)=\int_{\mathcal X^n}\mathfrak R_n(\pi,x)\,d\nu(x).$$
  Section 3 adds a measurability assumption ("We assume the signal function $\Phi(a,\cdot)$ is"
  $\mathfrak B(\mathcal X)/\mathfrak B(\Sigma)$-measurable).
- **Theorem 4.1 (minimax theorem).** "Let $\mathscr Q$ be the space of all finitely supported probability
  measures on $\mathcal X^n$. Then
  $$\inf_{\pi\in\Pi}\sup_{x\in\mathcal X^n}\mathfrak R_n(\pi,x)=\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr Q}\min_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathfrak{BR}_n(\pi,\nu)."$$
  Section 4 says the theorem "depends on no assumptions except that the action set is finite."
- **Bandit instance, Section 6 (summary).** "In the bandit setting the learner observes the loss of the
  action they play": $\Sigma=[0,1]$, $\mathcal X=[0,1]^k$ and $\Phi(a,x)=\mathcal L(a,x)=x_a$.
- **Theorem 6.1.** "The minimax regret for $k$-armed adversarial bandits satisfies
  $\mathfrak R^*_n\le\sqrt{2kn}$."
  Context: "The best known bound is by Bubeck and Cesa-Bianchi 2012", $\mathfrak R^*_n\le\sqrt{8kn}$;
  "Using the same potential in combination with Theorem 5.2 allows us to improve this result to"
  $\sqrt{2kn}$. The bound is non-constructive (it bounds the Bayesian regret of Thompson sampling
  for every prior and applies Theorem 4.1).
- **Stochastic vs adversarial (Section 2).** The paper does not state that the adversarial minimax
  regret bounds a stochastic one. The only sentence found on the relation: "Although a frequentist
  regret bound in a stochastic setting normally implies a Bayesian regret bound, in our Bayesian
  setup the environments are not stationary" (Section 2, related work).

## Techniques

- **Minimax theorem via compactness of randomised policies (Appendix A, "Proof of Theorem 4.1").**
  Policies are written as probability measures over deterministic policies $\Pi_D$; "By Tychonoff's
  theorem, $\Pi_D$ is compact with the product topology", and (citing Bogachev) $\mathcal P r(\Pi_D)$ is
  weak*-compact and convex; $\mathcal X^n$ carries the discrete topology and, "owning to the choice of
  $\mathscr Q$, the integral with respect to $\nu$ is a finite sum", so the payoff is "linear and
  continuous in both $\mu$ and $\nu$ individually"; the equality then follows "by Sion's minimax
  theorem ([Sion 1958])" (no statement or theorem number of Sion's result is given; footnote 1 notes
  that Sion's theorem only needs quasiconvexity/quasiconcavity and semicontinuity).
- **Information-ratio bound for Thompson sampling with the $1/2$-Tsallis potential (proof of
  Theorem 6.1).** "Let $F(p)=-2\sum_a\sqrt{p_a}$, which has domain $[0,\infty)^k$ and
  $\mathrm{diam}_F(\Delta^{k-1})\le2\sqrt k$"; the proof combines Corollary 5.4, Theorem 4.1 and
  Lemma 6.3. Generalises the information-theoretic analysis of Russo and Van Roy (2016).
