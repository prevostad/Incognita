## Setting

- The two arms are Bernoulli and "the sum of the means of the arms is one" (abstract: "the symmetric
  two-armed Bernoulli bandit"). The safe arm has mean $(1+\epsilon)/2$, the risky arm $(1-\epsilon)/2$
  (summary); "The suboptimality parameter or gap of the arms is given by" $\epsilon=\mu_j-\mu_i$. The
  player "knows that the distribution of the arms is symmetric"; footnote 4: "As the analysis below
  shows, an optimal player is the same for all feasible values of the gap ϵ."
- (1.8): $R_T^*:=\min_p\max_{j\in[2]}R_T(p,a(j))$, the adversary choosing which arm is safe ($j$), for
  a given gap; "Accordingly, a *minimax optimal player* $p^*$ is a minimizer of the *minimax regret*".
  $R_T$ is the final-time expected regret (pseudoregret $\bar R_T$ is treated separately, (1.11)).

## Results

- **Abstract (v5), verbatim.** "This work addresses a version of the two-armed Bernoulli bandit problem
  where the sum of the means of the arms is one (the symmetric two-armed Bernoulli bandit). In a regime
  where the gap between these means goes to zero as the number of prediction periods approaches
  infinity, i.e., the difficulty of detecting the gap increases as the sample size increases, we obtain
  the leading order terms of the minmax optimal regret and pseudoregret for this problem by associating
  each of them with a solution of a linear heat equation. Our results improve upon the previously known
  results; specifically, we explicitly compute these leading order terms in three different scaling
  regimes for the gap. Additionally, we obtain new non-asymptotic bounds for any given time horizon.
  Although optimal player strategies are not known for more general bandit problems, there is
  significant interest in considering how regret accumulates under specific player strategies, even
  when they are not known to be optimal. We expect that the methods of this paper should be useful in
  settings of that type."
- **Lemma 3.1, verbatim.** "The player $p^m$ given by Eq. 3.3 is a minimizer of Eq. 1.8 and Eq. 1.11.
  Moreover, this strategy makes the player indifferent about which arm is risky, that is,
  $R_T(p^m,a(1))=R_T(p^m,a(2))$ and $\bar R_T(p^m,a(1))=\bar R_T(p^m,a(2))$." ($p^m$ is the myopic
  player; introduction: "We show that the optimal player in the symmetric two-armed Bernoulli bandit
  problem in the minimax setting is the same as in the Bayesian setting described above.")
- **Theorem 3.4, verbatim.** "Let the functions $u$ and $v$ be as defined above, where $u$ is $C^1$ of
  (3.14), i.e., $\sigma=\kappa$ and $b=\sigma/\epsilon=\kappa/\epsilon$. Then,
  $|u(0,\epsilon T\mathbb 1,-T)-v(0,\epsilon T\mathbb 1,-T)|\le E_1(-T)$ where the error term $E_1(-T)$
  is $O\big(1+\sqrt\kappa+\big(\frac\kappa\epsilon\nu(\epsilon)+\frac\kappa\epsilon\rho(\epsilon)+1\big)T\big)$
  and $\rho(\epsilon)=\big(\exp(-\frac{2\epsilon}{\kappa})-1\big)(1+\epsilon)/2$,
  $\nu(\epsilon)=\big(\exp(-\frac{2\epsilon}{\kappa})(1+\epsilon)/2+\exp(\frac{2\epsilon}{\kappa})(1-\epsilon)/2-1\big)$."
  It continues: "When $\epsilon\to0$, the leading order terms ... are $\frac\kappa\epsilon\nu(\epsilon)\approx2\epsilon^3$
  and $\frac\kappa\epsilon\rho(\epsilon)+1\approx\epsilon^2$. Therefore, $E_1(T)$ is $O(1+\epsilon^2T)$."
  Here $\kappa=1-\epsilon^2$, $v(\eta,\xi,t)$ is "the final-time regret if the bandit game starts at time
  $t$" under $p^m$, and the paper states $R_T^*=R_T(p^m,a)=v(0,\epsilon T\mathbb 1,-T)$; $u$ solves the
  heat equation $u_t+Lu=q$, $Lu:=\frac\kappa2\Delta_\xi u$, with a discontinuous source $q$ (summary).
- **Corollary 3.5, (3.21), verbatim.** "For $\gamma=\epsilon\sqrt T$, if the leading order term of $b$ is
  $\frac1\epsilon$ as $\epsilon\to0$, we have
  $c(\gamma):=\lim_{\epsilon\to0}\frac1{\sqrt T}u(0,\epsilon T\mathbb 1,-T)=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}e^{-\gamma^2}+\gamma\,\mathrm{erf}(\gamma)+\big(\frac1\gamma-\gamma\big)\mathrm{erf}\big(\frac\gamma{\sqrt2}\big)-\sqrt{\frac2\pi}e^{-\frac{\gamma^2}2}$."
  Numerically, $c$ has "a unique maximizer $\gamma\approx.707$", giving "the maximum leading order
  regret $\approx.572\sqrt T$" (centered problem, rewards $\pm1$).
- **Introduction, verbatim.** "the largest regret of $.286\sqrt T$ (or $.572\sqrt T$ in the equivalent
  centered problem described below) is achieved when the safe arm has mean $1/2+.353/\sqrt T$ (or
  $.707/\sqrt T$ in the centered problem)".
- Small-gap regime $\epsilon=o(T^{-1/2})$: leading regret $\sqrt{T/\pi}$ (centered); pseudoregret
  results in three regimes (Theorems 3.9, 3.11): summary only, not read.

## Techniques

The minimax regret of the symmetric problem is achieved by the myopic player (Lemma 3.1), which
reduces the game to the regret of one explicit strategy; that value function solves a discrete
dynamic-programming recursion, compared with the solution of a linear heat equation with a
discontinuous source through consistency/stability estimates (Theorem 3.4), giving the leading order
term and non-asymptotic error bounds. Credits the Bayesian analysis of the same problem to its
reference [2] (bibliographic line not read).
