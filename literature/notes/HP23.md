## Results

- **Abstract (v2), verbatim.** "We develop asymptotic approximations that can be applied to sequential
  estimation and inference problems, adaptive randomized controlled trials, and related settings. In
  batched adaptive settings where the decision at one stage can affect the observation of variables in
  later stages, our asymptotic representation characterizes all limit distributions attainable through
  a joint choice of an adaptive design rule and statistics applied to the adaptively generated data.
  This facilitates local power analysis of tests, comparison of adaptive treatments rules, and other
  analyses of batchwise sequential statistical decision rules."
- **Setting of §4.2 (v2), verbatim.** "We assume there are $K$ treatment arms denoted $k=0,\dots,K-1$, and
  $B$ batches $b=1,\dots,B$." ... "Here $B$ and $K$ are both finite and fixed in advance" (sentence
  continues, not read); parametric DQM models $p^{(b)}_{\theta,k}$, local parameters
  $\theta_n(h)=\theta_0+h/\sqrt n$; Fisher informations $J^{(b)}_k$ need not be invertible. Footnote 3
  begins "In principle the number of periods B could be arbitrarily large" (the asymptotics are in the
  batch sizes, not in $B$; summary).
- **Theorem 2 (v2), verbatim pieces.** "Suppose that Assumption 1(a) (DQM) holds for each
  $p^{(b)}_{\theta,k}$ at $\theta_0$ with Fisher information $J^{(b)}_k$." "Suppose that, for every
  $h\in\mathbb R^m$, we have joint weak convergence under $\theta_n(h)=\theta_0+h/\sqrt n$:
  $(S_{1,n},\Lambda_{2,n},S_{2,n},\dots,\Lambda_{B,n},S_{B,n})\overset{h}{\leadsto}(S_1,\Lambda_2,S_2,\dots,\Lambda_B,S_B)$."
  Conclusion: there are $Z_1,\dots,Z_B$, $U\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]$ and statistics with
  "$Z_{1,0},\dots,Z_{1,K-1}$ and $U$ are independent" and
  $Z_{1,k}\sim N\big((\lambda_{1,k+1}-\lambda_{1,k})J^{(1)}_kh,(\lambda_{1,k+1}-\lambda_{1,k})J^{(1)}_k\big)$;
  for $b=2,\dots,B$, conditionally on $Z_1,\dots,Z_{b-1},U$, independent components
  $Z_{b,k}\sim N\big((\lambda_{b,k+1}-\lambda_{b,k})J^{(b)}_kh,(\lambda_{b,k+1}-\lambda_{b,k})J^{(b)}_k\big)$
  "where $(\lambda_{b,0},\dots,\lambda_{b,K})=T_{\Lambda_b}(Z_1,\dots,Z_{b-1},U)$"; and
  $(T_{S_1},T_{\Lambda_2},T_{S_2},\dots,T_{\Lambda_B},T_{S_B})\overset d=(S_1,\Lambda_2,S_2,\dots,\Lambda_B,S_B)$,
  "where the equality in distribution holds for every value of $h$." One line: with a fixed number $B$
  of batches and $K$ arms, every limit law of (allocations, statistics) of a batched experiment is the
  law of a batchwise Gaussian experiment whose batch-$b$ information is the allocated fraction times
  $J^{(b)}_k$.
- **§5.3 and §6 (v2), short quotations.** §5.3 treats "a batchwise version of the classic two-armed
  bandit problem" with allocations evaluated "through their welfare regret loss"; it compares rules
  (e.g. "c=1 (conventional Thompson sampling) has the lowest worst-case risk" among those considered)
  and notes a Bayes property under a flat prior; it does not derive a minimax regret value. §6: the
  representation "can be combined with any solution concept such as minmax regret or robust
  preferences". The word "minimax" does not occur in the part read.

## Techniques

Limits of experiments (Le Cam) for adaptive, batchwise designs: joint weak convergence of
log-likelihood ratios, allocations and statistics, and a representation by a sequence of Gaussian
shift experiments with data-dependent (randomised through $U$) allocation fractions (Theorem 2;
single-armed intuition in §4.1).
