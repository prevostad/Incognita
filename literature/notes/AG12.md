## Results

Mathematics is transcribed into LaTeX from the page images.

- **Numbering.** In the PMLR version, the rounding trick is in **Section 1.2 ("Thompson Sampling"), pp. 39.3–39.4**,
  with **Algorithm 2** on p. 39.4. Section 2 is "Proof Techniques" (p. 39.5) and does not describe the rounding; it
  only says: "We assume that all arms are Bernoulli arms, and that the first arm is the unique optimal arm. As
  explained in the previous sections, these assumptions are without loss of generality."
- **Setting, Section 1.1, p. 39.2.** "Each arm $i$, when played, yields a random real-valued reward according to some
  fixed (unknown) distribution with support in $[0,1]$."
- **The adaptation, Section 1.2, p. 39.3, verbatim.** "We adapt the Bernoulli Thompson sampling algorithm to the general
  stochastic bandits case, i.e. when the rewards for arm $i$ are generated from an arbitrary unknown distribution with
  support $[0,1]$ and mean $\mu_i$, in a way that allows us to reuse our analysis of the Bernoulli case. To our
  knowledge, this adaptation is new. We modify TS so that after observing the reward $\tilde r_t\in[0,1]$ at time $t$,
  it performs a Bernoulli trial with success probability $\tilde r_t$. Let random variable $r_t$ denote the outcome of
  this Bernoulli trial, and let $\{S_i(t),F_i(t)\}$ denote the number of successes and failures in the Bernoulli
  trials until time $t$. The remaining algorithm is the same as for Bernoulli bandits. Algorithm 2 gives the precise
  description of this algorithm."
- **Success probability equals the mean, Section 1.2, p. 39.3, verbatim.** "We observe that the probability of
  observing a success (i.e., $r_t=1$) in the Bernoulli trial after playing an arm $i$ in the new generalized algorithm
  is equal to the mean reward $\mu_i$. Let $f_i$ denote the (unknown) pdf of reward distribution for arm $i$. Then, on
  playing arm $i$,
  $$\Pr(r_t=1)=\int_0^1\tilde rf_i(\tilde r)d\tilde r=\mu_i."$$
- **Algorithm 2, p. 39.4, verbatim.** Caption "**Algorithm 2** Thompson Sampling for general stochastic bandits".
  "For each arm $i=1,\dots,N$ set $S_i(1)=0,F_i(1)=0$. / **foreach** $t=1,2,\dots,$ **do** / For each arm
  $i=1,\dots,N$, sample $\theta_i(t)$ from the Beta$(S_i+1,F_i+1)$ distribution. / Play arm $i(t):=\arg\max_i\theta_i(t)$
  and observe reward $\tilde r_t$. / **Perform a Bernoulli trial with success probability $\tilde r_t$ and observe
  output $r_t$.** / If $r_t=1$, then $S_{i(t)}=S_{i(t)}+1$, else $F_{i(t)}=F_{i(t)}+1$. / **end**" (the Bernoulli-trial
  line is in bold in the original).
- **After Algorithm 2, p. 39.4, verbatim.** "Thus, the probability of observing $r_t=1$ is same and $S_i(t),F_i(t)$
  evolve exactly in the same way as in the case of Bernoulli bandits with mean $\mu_i$. Therefore, the analysis of TS
  for Bernoulli setting is applicable to this modified TS for the general setting. This allows us to replace, for the
  purpose of analysis, the problem with general stochastic bandits with Bernoulli bandits with the same means."
- Also p. 39.4: "We remark that instead of using $r_t$, we could consider more direct and natural updates of type
  Beta$(\alpha_i,\beta_i)$ to Beta$(\alpha_i+\tilde r_t,\beta_i+1-\tilde r_t)$. However, we do not know how to analyze
  this because of our essential use of Fact 1, which requires $\alpha_i,\beta_i$ to be integral."

## Relevance to P001

`papers/P001-minimax-constant/paper.tex`, proof of Theorem `thm:bounds`, upper bound ("From adversarial to stochastic
rewards", "The policy", "Its regret"): the policy $\tilde\pi$ feeds the adversarial policy of `LS19` Theorem 6.1 the
binary losses $b_s:=\1\{V_s\le1-x_s\}$, with $V_s$ uniform and independent, and uses
$\P(V_t\le1-Y_{t,k}\mid Y_{t,k})=1-Y_{t,k}$. This is the AG12 rounding applied to the loss $1-x$: $b$ is a Bernoulli
trial with success probability equal to the loss, equivalently $1-b=\1\{V>1-x\}$ is a Bernoulli trial with success
probability the reward $x$ (Algorithm 2 above), whose conditional mean is the reward, so its mean is $\mu_k$. AG12
uses it to reduce rewards in $[0,1]$ to Bernoulli rewards for Thompson sampling. In P001 it serves to feed a policy
only $\{0,1\}$-valued losses, so that its histories form a finite set (a measurability point AG12 does not raise).
Suggested citation: `\cite[Section~1.2, Algorithm~2]{AG12}`; the referee's "Section 2" does not match the PMLR
numbering.
