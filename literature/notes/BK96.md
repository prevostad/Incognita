### Model (§1, §2, pp. 122–124)

- "Consider for any $a=1,\ldots,m$ the i.i.d. random variables $Y_a,Y_{aj}$, $j=1,2,\ldots$ with
  univariate density function $f_a(y,\underline\theta_a)$, with respect to some known measure
  $\nu_a$, where $\underline\theta_a$ is a vector of unknown parameters $(\theta_{a1},\ldots,\theta_{ak_a})$.
  For each $a$, $k_a$ is known and the vector $\underline\theta_a$ belongs to some known set
  $\Theta_a$ that in general depends on $a$ and is a subset of $\mathbb R^{k_a}$." The parameter is
  $\underline\theta=(\underline\theta_1,\ldots,\underline\theta_m)\in\Theta:=\Theta_1\times\cdots\times\Theta_m$.
- Counts (p. 124): "$T_n(a):=\sum_{t=0}^{n-1}\mathbf 1\{\pi_t=a\}$"; time starts at $t=0$.

### Definitions (1)–(5), §2.2, p. 125 (verbatim)

"(1) (a) $\mu_a(\underline\theta_a):=\mathbf E_{\underline\theta_a}Y_a$,
(b) $\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a):=\mathbf E_{\underline\theta_a}\log(f_a(Y_a;\underline\theta_a)/f_a(Y_a;\underline\theta'_a))$,
(2) (a) $\mu^*=\mu^*(\underline\theta):=\max_{a=1,\ldots,m}\{\mu_a(\underline\theta_a)\}$,
(b) $\mathbf O(\underline\theta):=\{a:\mu_a=\mu^*(\underline\theta)\}$,
(3) (a) $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a,\varepsilon):=\{\underline\theta'_a\in\Theta_a:\ \mu_a(\underline\theta'_a)>\mu^*(\underline\theta)-\varepsilon\}$, for $\varepsilon>0$,
(b) $\mathbf w_a(\underline\theta_a,z):=\inf\{\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a):\ \mu_a(\underline\theta'_a)>z\}$, for $-\infty\le z\le\infty$,
(c) $\mathbf J_a(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta;\varepsilon):=\mathbf w_a(\underline\theta_a,\mu^*(\underline\theta)-\varepsilon)=\inf\{\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a):\ \underline\theta'_a\in\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a,\varepsilon)\}$, for $\varepsilon\ge0$,
(4) (a) $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a):=\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a,0)=\{\underline\theta'_a\in\Theta_a:\ \mu_a(\underline\theta'_a)>\mu^*(\underline\theta)\}$,
(b) $\mathbf B(\underline\theta):=\{a:\ a\notin\mathbf O(\underline\theta)$ and $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a)\ne\emptyset\}$,
(5) (a) $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta):=\mathbf J_a(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta;0)=\inf\{\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a):\ \underline\theta'_a\in\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a)\}$, for $a\in\mathbf B(\underline\theta)$,
(b) $\mathbf M(\underline\theta):=\sum_{a\in\mathbf B(\underline\theta)}(\mu^*(\underline\theta)-\mu_a(\underline\theta_a))/\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)$.
In the definition of $\mathbf M(\underline\theta)$ we have used the fact that $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)\in(0,\infty)$,
$\forall a\in\mathbf B(\underline\theta)\subseteq\bar{\mathbf O}(\underline\theta)$, which is a consequence of the fact that
$\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a)=0$ only when $\underline\theta_a=\underline\theta'_a$."

**The inequality on the mean is strict** in (3)(a), (3)(b) and (4)(a): $\mathbf K_a$ is the infimum of
$\mathbf I$ over parameters of mean $>\mu^*(\underline\theta)$, within the same parametric family
$\Theta_a$. (Clause (3)(a) is stated "for $\varepsilon>0$" while (3)(c) and (4)(a) use $\varepsilon\ge0$,
as printed.) Page 126: "Under the assumptions made in[25] the constant $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)$
reduces to $\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\theta^*)$, thus giving the form for $\mathbf M(\underline\theta)$ used
in that paper." ([25] is Lai–Robbins 1985.)

### Optimality criteria, index, Theorem 1 (§2.3, §3, pp. 126–129)

- Regret $R^\pi_n(\underline\theta)=V_n(\underline\theta)-V^\pi_n(\underline\theta)$ with
  $V_n(\underline\theta)=n\mu^*(\underline\theta)$. "A policy $\pi$ will be called *uniformly convergent*
  (UC) or *uniformly fast convergent* (UF) if $\forall\underline\theta\in\Theta$ as $n\to\infty$,
  $R^\pi_n(\underline\theta)=o(n)$ (for UC) or $R^\pi_n(\underline\theta)=o(n^\alpha)$, $\forall\alpha>0$ (for UF)."
- Index (3.1)–(3.2): "$\mathbf u_a(\underline\theta_a,\gamma)=\sup_{\underline\theta'_a\in\Theta_a}\{\mu_a(\underline\theta'_a):\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta'_a)<\gamma\}$"
  and "$\mathbf U_a(\omega_k)=\mathbf u_a(\hat{\underline\theta}_a,\log k/T_k(a))$" (note the strict
  "$<\gamma$").
- Conditions A1–A3 (p. 127) are the sufficient conditions for Theorem 1 (2); A1: "$\forall\underline\theta\in\Theta$
  and $\forall a\notin\mathbf O(\underline\theta)$ such that $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a,0)=\emptyset$ and
  $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta_a,\varepsilon)\ne\emptyset$, $\forall\varepsilon>0$, the following relation holds:
  $\lim_{\varepsilon\to0}\mathbf J_a(\underline\theta_a,\underline\theta;\varepsilon)=\infty$." A2 is a rate $o(1/k)$ for
  $\mathbf P_{\underline\theta_a}(\|\hat{\underline\theta}^k_a-\underline\theta_a\|>\varepsilon)$; A3 is
  "$\mathbf P_{\underline\theta_a}(\mathbf u_a(\hat{\underline\theta}^j_a,\log k/j)<\mu(\underline\theta_a)-\varepsilon$, for some
  $j\le k)=o(1/k)$, as $k\to\infty$, $\forall\varepsilon>0$, $\forall\underline\theta_a\in\Theta_a$, $\forall a$."
- **Theorem 1 (1)** (p. 128): "For any $\underline\theta\in\Theta$, $a\in\mathbf B(\underline\theta)$, such that
  $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)\ne0$ the following is true, $\forall\pi\in C_{\mathrm{UF}}$,
  $\underline{\lim}_{n\to\infty}\mathbf E^\pi_{\underline\theta}T_n(a)/\log n\ge1/\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)$."
  **(2)** (p. 129): "If conditions (A1), (A2), and (A3) hold and $\pi^0\in C_R$, then,
  $\forall\underline\theta\in\Theta$ and $\forall a\notin\varnothing(\underline\theta)$," (as printed; presumably
  $\mathbf O(\underline\theta)$) (a) $\overline{\lim}\,\mathbf E^{\pi^0}_{\underline\theta}T_n(a)/\log n\le1/\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)$ if
  $a\in\mathbf B(\underline\theta)$, $=0$ if $a\notin\mathbf B(\underline\theta)$; (b)
  $0\le R^{\pi^0}_n(\underline\theta)=\mathbf M(\underline\theta)\log n+o(\log n)$; (c) $C_R\subset C_{\mathrm{UM}}$. Parts (1) and
  (2a) are proved in Propositions 1 and 2 (not read). $C_R$: policies that always play an arm of
  largest $\mathbf U_a(\omega_k)$.

### §4.2 Normal distributions (known variance), pp. 139–140

"Assume that the observations $Y_{aj}$ from population $a$ are normally distributed with unknown
mean $\mu_a$ and known variance $\sigma_a^2$, i.e., $\underline\theta_a=\mu_a$, and that
$\Theta_a=(-\infty,\infty)$." The formulas for $\mathbf K$ and $\mathbf U_a(\omega_k)$ printed in §4.2,
at the resolution of the page image, look identical to those of §4.4 below (a $\log$ form with
$\hat\sigma_a$), which does not fit the known-variance model; not checked at higher resolution, and
not used here.

### §4.4 Normal Distributions with Unknown Variance, p. 140 (verbatim)

"Assume that $Y_{aj}$ are normally distributed with unknown mean $\mu_a$ and variance $\sigma_a^2$,
i.e., $\underline\theta_a=(\mu_a,\sigma_a^2)$, and that $\Theta_a=\{\underline\theta_a:\ \mu_a\in\mathbb R,\ \sigma_a^2\ge0\}$.
Given history $\omega_k$, define $\hat{\underline\theta}{}_a^{T_k(a)}=(\hat\mu_a^{T_k(a)},\hat\sigma_a^{2\,T_k(a)})$, where
$\hat\mu_a^{T_k(a)}=1/T_k(a)\sum_{j=1}^{T_k(a)}Y_{aj}$, $\hat\sigma_a^{2\,T_k(a)}=s^2(T_k(a))=1/T_k(a)\sum_{j=1}^{T_k(a)}(Y_{aj}-\hat\mu_a^{T_k(a)})^2$.
From the definition of $\Theta_a$ it follows that $\Delta\Theta_a(\underline\theta)\ne\emptyset$, $\forall\underline\theta\in\Theta$;
therefore, $\mathbf B(\underline\theta)=\{1,\ldots,m\}$, $\forall\underline\theta\in\Theta$. Also it can be seen after some algebra
that $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)=(1/2)\log(1+(\mu^*(\underline\theta)-\mu_a)^2/\sigma_a^2)$ and
$\mathbf U_a(\omega_k)=\hat\mu_a^{T_k(a)}+\hat\sigma_a^{T_k(a)}(e^{2\log k/T_k(a)}-1)^{1/2}=\hat\mu_a^{T_k(a)}+\hat\sigma_a^{T_k(a)}(k^{2/T_k(a)}-1)^{1/2}$.
Therefore, Condition (A1) of Theorem 1 holds. It is easy to to see that (A2) holds, using large
deviations arguments. However, we have not been able to prove that (A3) is satisfied, so this
remains an open problem."

(The doubled "to" is printed. $\Theta_a$ allows $\sigma_a^2=0$ as printed; $\mathbf B(\underline\theta)$
includes every arm, also the optimal ones, as printed. The variance estimate is the biased one,
divided by $T_k(a)$.)

## Techniques

- $\mathbf K_a(\underline\theta)$, the minimal Kullback–Leibler information to a parameter of the same
  family with a strictly larger mean than $\mu^*(\underline\theta)$, replaces Lai–Robbins'
  $\mathbf I(\underline\theta_a,\theta^*)$ for multi-parameter (and non-parametric discrete) families;
  lower bound for UF policies (Theorem 1 (1)); index policies $C_R$ defined by the dual problem
  (3.1) (duality with $\mathbf w_a$, Remark 2(c), p. 127, credited to the finance literature [32, Varian]).
- p. 123: the authors "first discovered the form of the indices used in this paper when we employed
  the dynamic programming approach to study a Bayes version of this problem [6, 7]", and relate
  their work to [33] (Yakowitz–Lowe 1991).
