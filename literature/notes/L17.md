## Results

Statements copied from the text layer and the ar5iv rendering, and compared on 2026-11-20 with the
page images of the arXiv v1 PDF; formulas rebuilt in LaTeX with the paper's symbols ($\mu^*$ the
largest mean, $T_i(n)$ the number of pulls of arm $i$, $\mathcal R_n=\mathbf E[\sum_{t=1}^n\Delta_{A_t}]$
the regret, (1); the source writes $\mathbf E$, $\mathbf V$ for expectation and variance, and $\mathcal R_n$,
calligraphic, where this note writes $R_n$).

- **Assumption 1.** "There exists a known $\kappa\in\mathbb R$ such that for all $1\le i\le k$, the kurtosis of
  $X\sim\nu_i$ is at most $\mathrm{Kurt}[X]=\frac{\mathbb E[(X-\mathbb E[X])^4]}{\mathbb V[X]^2}\le\kappa$."
- **Theorem 2.** "If Assumption 1 holds, then the algorithm described in §2 satisfies
  $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{R_n}{\log(n)}\le C\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}\Delta_i\Big(\kappa-1+\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\Delta_i^2}\Big)$,
  where $\sigma_i^2$ is the variance of $\nu_i$ and $C>0$ is a universal constant." (The algorithm of §2 is
  based on median-of-means estimators; not transcribed.)
- **Definition (§3).** "$\mathcal H_\kappa=\{\nu:\nu$ has kurtosis less than $\kappa\}$." (Strict inequality,
  whereas Assumption 1 is non-strict.)
- **Inequality (6).** "Burnetas and Katehakis [1996] showed that for any consistent strategy
  $\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb E[T_i(n)]}{\log(n)}\ge\sup\Big\{\frac{1}{\mathrm{KL}(\nu_i,\nu_i')}:\nu'\in\mathcal H\text{ and }\mathbb E_{X\sim\nu_i'}[X]>\mu^*\Big\}$."
- **Theorem 9.** "Let $\kappa_\circ\ge7/2$ and $\Delta>0$ and $\nu\in\mathcal H_{\kappa_\circ}$ with mean $\mu$, variance
  $\sigma^2>0$ and kurtosis $\kappa$. Then
  $\inf\{\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\nu'):\nu'\in\mathcal H_\kappa\text{ and }\mathbb E_{X\sim\nu'}[X]>\mu+\Delta\}\le\min\{\log\frac1{1-p},\frac{C'\Delta^2}{\sigma^2}\}$
  if $C\kappa^{1/2}(\kappa+1)\frac{\Delta}{\sigma}<\kappa_\circ$, and $\le\log\frac1{1-p}$ otherwise, where
  $C,C'>0$ are universal constants and $p=\min\{\Delta/\sigma,1/\kappa_\circ\}$."
  *Remark (not in the source):* the class of the alternatives is printed $\mathcal H_\kappa$, with $\kappa$ the
  kurtosis of $\nu$; read literally it would not contain $\nu$ itself. The proof (Appendix A) builds the
  alternative law of $Z=X+\Delta B/p$ and shows $\mathrm{Kurt}[Z]\le\kappa_\circ$, so the statement proved is
  the one with alternatives of kurtosis at most $\kappa_\circ$. P005 restates the theorem with the
  alternatives in the class with parameter $\kappa_\circ$, which is the weaker reading (a larger class of
  alternatives). Checked on the page images (2026-11-20): the class of the alternatives is printed
  $\mathcal H_\kappa$ (p. 8), and Appendix A (p. 11) ends its first idea with $\mathrm{Kurt}[Z]\le\kappa_\circ$, by
  Lemma 3 and "the assumption that $\kappa_\circ\ge7/2$". Appendix A then prints "Let $\nu'=\mathcal L(Y)$ be
  the law of $Y$. Then $\mathrm{KL}(\nu,\nu')\le\log\big(\frac1{1-p}\big)$" (p. 12), where $Y=\Delta B/p$; the
  alternative whose kurtosis was just bounded, and whose mean is $\Delta$, is the law of $Z=X+Y$, so $Y$ there
  reads as a slip for $Z$ (as printed; the bound $\log\frac1{1-p}$ is then the divergence of $\nu$ from the
  mixture $(1-p)\nu+p\,\nu(\cdot-\Delta/p)$). The proof normalises $\nu$ to mean $0$ and variance $1$, so
  $p=\min\{\Delta,1/\kappa_\circ\}$ there.
- **Paragraph after Theorem 9.** The bound is strongest when the kurtosis of $\nu$ is much smaller than
  $\kappa_\circ$; a Bernoulli law of kurtosis $\kappa_\circ$ leaves "very little wiggle room"; with (6) the
  lower bound matches Theorem 2 up to constant factors when $\kappa$ and $\Delta$ are small relative to
  $\kappa_\circ$.
- **The classes of §3 (p. 7).** "We need two sets of distributions on $\mathbf R$.
  $\mathcal H_\sigma=\{\nu:\nu$ is $\sigma^2$-subgaussian$\}$. $\mathcal H_\kappa=\{\nu:\nu$ has kurtosis less than $\kappa\}$."
  Consistency: "a bandit strategy is called consistent over a set of distributions $\mathcal H$ if
  $\mathcal R_n=o(n^p)$ for all $p\in(0,1)$ and bandits in $\mathcal H^k$"; a bandit is non-trivial "if
  there exists a suboptimal arm".
- **Theorem 7 (p. 7).** "Suppose there exists a $\sigma>0$ and non-trivial bandit $\{\nu_i\}\in\mathcal H_\sigma^k$
  such that $\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal R_n}{\log(n)}<\infty$. Then the strategy is not consistent over
  $\bigcup_{\sigma\ge0}\mathcal H_\sigma^k$." The text before it: "The proof is quite standard and is simply
  omitted."
  **Correction (2026-11-20).** Until this date this note described Theorem 7 as a statement on the
  kurtosis classes ("a non-trivial bandit of some kurtosis class … the union of the classes"). As printed,
  Theorem 7 is on the *sub-Gaussian* classes $\mathcal H_\sigma$: the bandit is $\sigma^2$-subgaussian and the
  union is over $\sigma\ge0$. The kurtosis classes $\mathcal H_\kappa$ appear in the source only in Theorem 9
  and in the sentence of §4 "Comparison to Bernoulli" (p. 10) that logarithmic regret on a class with
  infinite kurtosis "is not possible in general, as demonstrated by Theorem 7". The statement "a strategy
  with logarithmic regret on some non-trivial bandit of kurtosis at most $\kappa$ is not consistent over the
  union over $\kappa$ of these classes" is not printed in the source, and it does not follow from Theorem 7
  by inclusion: its hypothesis allows a bandit that is not sub-Gaussian, and $\bigcup_{\sigma\ge0}\mathcal H_\sigma$
  contains the Dirac masses, which have no kurtosis, so it is not contained in $\bigcup_\kappa\mathcal H_\kappa$.
- **Remark 8 (p. 7).** There are consistent strategies over $\bigcup_{\sigma\ge0}\mathcal H_\sigma^k$: the index
  $\hat\mu_i(t-1)+\sqrt{f(t)/T_i(T-1)}$ (as printed) with $f$ monotone increasing, $f(t)=\omega(\log t)$ and
  $f(t)=o(t^p)$ for all $p\in(0,1)$, satisfies $\limsup_n\mathcal R_n/f(n)=\sum_{i:\Delta_i>0}1/\Delta_i^2$ for every
  bandit in $\mathcal H_\sigma^k$ (following Bubeck and Cesa-Bianchi 2012, Chapter 2); the constants hidden in the
  asymptotics depend on $\tau=\min\{t:f(t)\ge2\sigma^2\log(t)\}$.
- **§4 (Summary), "Optimal constants".** The leading constants of Theorems 2 and 9 are loose; deriving the
  optimal form of the regret is called an interesting challenge; the author suggests that "it may be
  necessary to resort to an implicit analysis" showing whether (6) is achievable on the bounded-kurtosis
  class, and adds that constructing an efficient algorithm would remain a challenge. Also open there:
  a non-parametric Thompson sampling for this class.
- **Lemma 3 (p. 4; the source says before it that it "is well known").** "Let $X_1,X_2$ be independent
  random variables with $X_i$ having variance $\sigma_i^2$ and kurtosis $\kappa_i<\infty$ and skewness
  $\gamma_i=\mathbf E[(X_i-\mathbf E[X_i])^3/\sigma_i^3]$, then: (a) $\mathrm{Kurt}[X_1+X_2]=3+\frac{\sigma_1^4(\kappa_1-3)+\sigma_2^4(\kappa_2-3)}{(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)^2}$.
  (b) $\gamma_1\le\sqrt{\kappa_1-1}$." No proof is printed.

## Techniques

- Perturbation by a Bernoulli "outlier", $Z=X+\Delta B/p$ with $B\sim$ Bernoulli($p$) independent of $X$
  (Appendix A, first idea), and a smooth perturbation $(1+g)\,d\nu$ with $g$ affine on a bounded set
  (second idea), to bound the infimum of (6) from above.
- Median-of-means estimators of mean and variance in a scale-free index (§2, not transcribed).
